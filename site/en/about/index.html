About · Christian Bucher · Vienna

A career changer who measures.

I direct AI coding agents to ship tested software — and I measure whether what I built actually worked.

Story

I do not come from computer science. I sold, advised and dispatched trucks — jobs where you explain technical things to non-experts every day. In February 2026 I started building with Claude Code. First wrongly: hundreds of generated pages, shops without goods. Since July, better — with relapses I do not hide.

I direct agents to software that passes tests and is shipped — an MCP memory server on npm, plus small verification tools for agents; one checks whether an agent really did what it reports, one whether its behaviour changed after a model switch.

The biggest lesson came from measuring. In August a system prompt I had built seemed to show a large effect on a public benchmark. An adversarial review round of my own agents — following a rule I had set three days earlier — found two flaws in my setup the same day, only one of them fixable. I withdrew the number before it left the project. Since then I require a placebo arm, test the grader as hard as the candidate, and say when something does not work.

That is why I am useful to teams: I show how to direct agents — and when not to trust them.

Principles

  • Verify, don't trust the report. Evidence: postcondition-mcp checks the state of the world, not the success message.
  • Every number with a source. Evidence: every number on this site is listed with its source and verification date in facts.json; a linter lets no other through.
  • Withdraw what does not hold. Evidence: the case study.
  • Limits before strengths. Evidence: every project on the projects page states what it does not do.
