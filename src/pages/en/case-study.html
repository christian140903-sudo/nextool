Anatomy of a phantom effect

I measure whether it really works — even when the result goes against me.

As of {{date:2026-10-08}} · raw data not yet published · long version to follow · {{todo:Chriso reviews and puts this into his own words}}

What I measured

In August 2026 I tested whether my own system prompt improves language models on the public HumanEval coding benchmark. The comparison was paired, on two open models from the gpt-oss family. I committed the prediction for the second run beforehand ({{time:2026-08-28T11:57|11:57}}, the run followed at {{time:2026-08-28T12:25|12:25}}, on {{date:2026-08-28}}). A placebo arm with equal-length filler text was built, but its runs stayed incomplete ({{fact:case.placebo_runs}} tasks).

What the review found

An audit of the stored answers and an adversarial review round of my own agents — following a rule I had set three days earlier — found two flaws in my setup the same day:

  1. The grader took the first code block of an answer. When a model wrote a formula first and the solution second, the formula was graded. This flaw was fixable.
  2. Only the arm with my prompt was told to answer strictly in the required format. This flaw is in the runs themselves; it could not be fixed afterwards.

I registered {{fact:case.registered}} and withdrew them the same day, before the number left the project. From the stored answers only {{fact:case.recomputable}} can be recomputed — the difference is unexplained. After fixing the grader, {{fact:case.after_fix}} remained ({{fact:case.after_fix_p}}) on runs that were still unequally instructed; the experiment could not be corrected, only discarded. Raw data not yet published.

What I cannot explain

The registered and withdrawn number ({{fact:case.registered}}) cannot be recomputed exactly from the stored answers; they give {{fact:case.recomputable}}. I do not know where the difference comes from. I state it anyway, because a case study about measurement errors would otherwise hide one itself.

The second phantom effect: length instead of quality

Earlier comparisons in my evals had been scored by an AI judge. An audit showed that this judge mostly picked the longer answer: in a recomputation from the raw data, the longer answer wins {{fact:case.length_bias}} of the comparisons. Whether the judge rewards length or the longer answers really were better is open. I withdrew the advantages measured with this judge. Raw data not yet published.

What this case study does not show

  • The experiment was not pre-registered as a whole; only the prediction for the second run was fixed in advance.
  • It ran on two open models from the same family.
  • The p-value comes from a sign test over dependent runs and is correspondingly weak.
  • Who triggered the first audit is not documented.
  • Raw data, the lock file and the list of withdrawn numbers are not yet published.

What I do differently since

Since then I require a placebo arm, test the grader as hard as the candidate, and say when something does not work. In practice:

  • measure against the same amount of text, not against nothing;
  • diff the requests both variants actually send;
  • read the answers that were marked wrong;
  • before any number leaves the project, a counter-check whose only job is “find the error”.

Built with Claude agents under my direction; the worst flaw was found by the counter-check, not by me.
