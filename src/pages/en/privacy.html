Hosting and delivery via Cloudflare

What data: With every request, Cloudflare processes the data your browser sends for technical reasons: IP address, date and time, the address requested, browser identifier (user agent) and, if your browser sends it, the page you came from (referrer).

Purpose: To deliver the website, keep it available and protect it against attacks and abuse.

Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is a secure and reliably available website.

Cloudflare’s role: Cloudflare processes this data on my behalf under its Data Processing Addendum. For its own purposes, such as securing its network, Cloudflare acts as an independent controller under its privacy policy.

Transfers to the US: Cloudflare states that it is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Transfers to companies certified in this way are covered by an adequacy decision of the European Commission (Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795 of {{date:2023-07-10}}). If the certification lapses, the Data Processing Addendum provides for the Commission’s standard contractual clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914).

Retention: In my Cloudflare account I see aggregated statistics and, for requests that security features have checked or blocked, entries that include the IP address. These entries are available for at most {{fact:cloudflare.security_events_days}}. I do not use them to identify visitors. How long Cloudflare keeps data for its own purposes is set out in its privacy policy.

Links to other websites

This website links to other websites, for example GitHub and npm. Your browser only connects to them when you click such a link; it then usually sends only the address of this website as the origin, not the individual page. Their own privacy policies then apply.

Cookies and browser storage

Cookies are set only in one exceptional case: if Cloudflare shows a security check to protect the site against attacks, it sets the technically necessary cookie cf_clearance once the check has been passed. It records that you passed, so that the check is not repeated on every request, and with Cloudflare’s default setting it is valid for {{fact:cloudflare.cf_clearance_minutes}}. During the check, Cloudflare may set further cookies whose names begin with cf_chl_; Cloudflare uses them to detect technical problems with the check in the browser. The legal basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is protecting the website against attacks.

The website itself sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser’s local storage. The pages contain no scripts; whether you see the light or dark view follows your system setting, and the language follows from the address.

Earlier versions of this website left a service worker with a cache in some browsers. When such a browser visits the website again, the files /sw.js and /prep/sw.js replace it: they delete those caches, unregister the service worker and reload the open page. They analyse nothing and transmit no further data; the reload is an ordinary page request.

For storage and access in the browser that is strictly necessary to provide a service the user has explicitly asked for, Section 165(3) of the Austrian Telecommunications Act 2021 (Telekommunikationsgesetz 2021, TKG 2021) does not require consent.
